Abstract

ORDA models the process by which researchers apply to obtain access to platform data under Article 40 of the EU Digital Services Act (Regulation 2022/2065), for the purpose of studying systemic risks. It covers the actors involved, the request lifecycle, the data made available, and the safeguards attached to it.

The ontology's organising distinction is between the two access routes that Article 40 creates: the Art. 40(4) route, in which a request for non-public data is mediated by a Digital Services Coordinator (DSC), and the Art. 40(12) route, in which publicly accessible data is requested directly from the platform without intermediation.

3. Introduction

Article 40 of the Digital Services Act obliges providers of Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) and Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs) to give researchers access to data for studying systemic risks in the European Union. Delegated Regulation 2025/2050 sets out the procedure for the non-public (40(4)) data route. The process spans several actors (researchers and their institutions, the Digital Services Coordinator of establishment, the coordinator of the researcher's own member state, and the platform), and moves through a sequence of assessments with defined outcomes.

ORDA gives that process a machine-readable vocabulary. It types the requests, the parties, the assessments and their statuses, the data catalogues platforms must publish, and the technical, legal and organisational safeguards attached to an access modality. It reuses established vocabularies wherever one already covers the existing concepts (listed in section 12), and defines new terms only for what is specific to the Article 40 regime.

This draft scopes the model to the application stage: submitting a request, assessing it, and issuing the reasoned request that obliges the platform to supply data. Decision records, procedural deadlines, dispute settlement and research outputs are named in the legislation but are not yet modelled.

5.2 Deliberate non-disjointness

Four overlaps are intentional and should not be "fixed" by adding disjointness axioms.

  • orda:DSCofEstablishment and orda:DSCofMemberState, where the researcher's institution and the platform are established in the same member state, one authority holds both roles.
  • orda:LegalModality, orda:OrganisationalModality and orda:TechnicalModality: under Delegated ACT Art. 9 modalities combine. A secure enclave governed by a confidentiality undertaking is both technical and legal, and disjointness would force it to be split into two unrelated individuals.
  • orda:ApplicantResearcher and orda:VettedResearcher: a researcher named on an application may already hold vetted status. orda:PrincipalResearcher is a sub-class of the former, so the principal researcher is always among the applicants.
  • orda:NecessityStatement and orda:ProportionalityStatement: Art. 40(8)(e) states a single conjoint test, requiring the application to demonstrate that access and the time frames requested are "necessary for, and proportionate to" the research purpose. An application may discharge both limbs in one passage of text, so a single statement may carry both types; disjointness would force such a passage to be duplicated into two individuals.

orda:DataAccessRequest

A formal request by a researcher to access data from a VLOP or VLOSE under DSA Art. 40. Superclass for the two distinct processes: Art. 40(4) non-public data via DSC, and Art. 40(12) public data direct to platform. Every application must propose the period of access it seeks, via orda:accessStartDate and orda:accessEndDate, and must carry both limbs of the Art. 40(8)(e) demonstration via orda:hasAccessJustification. Both requirements sit on this superclass rather than on the Art. 40(4) sub-class because Art. 40(12) cross-references Art. 40(8) points (b) to (e), so the necessity and proportionality condition, and with it the demonstration of the time frames requested, binds on both routes.

orda:DataManagementPlan

A plan governing how accessed data is stored, protected and deleted. 13. Example Data

Illustrative instance data is distributed separately, in orda-examples.ttl: a worked Art. 40(4) application from a university researcher to a designated platform, with its status history, funding chain, and the CSV, JSON and PDF distributions returned.

It is kept out of the vocabulary file deliberately. Those individuals are described by ORDA rather than defined by it, so they carry no rdfs:isDefinedBy assertion and do not appear as terms in this document. The example file declares its own ontology IRI, https://w3id.org/orda/examples, and imports ORDA.

The example data satisfies every class restriction in this document concretely rather than vacuously: the worked application names a principal researcher who is affiliated with a university, the project names both its grant and the agency funding that grant, and the application carries the Art. 40(8)(g) publication commitment. It also proposes the period of access it seeks and carries both limbs of the Art. 40(8)(e) demonstration as separate orda:NecessityStatement and orda:ProportionalityStatement individuals, each with its text carried by dct:description. The reasoned request then carries a shorter access period than the application proposed, showing the distinction between a researcher proposing a period and the DSC determining one under Delegated ACT Art. 10.

It also exercises the account vocabulary: a dpv:NaturalPerson holds two accounts (one as:Person, one as:Application standing for an automated account), linked by orda:hasAccount. Activity-history instance data is not included while ORDA carries no history classes of its own; the collections reached by orda:containsHistory and orda:hasActivities are plain as:OrderedCollection instances.

14. Conformance Notes

Two limitations are worth stating plainly for anyone reusing this vocabulary.

Datatype profile. Six datatype properties range over xsd:date, which is not part of the OWL 2 datatype map. Reasoners that enforce the map, HermiT among them, will refuse to load the ontology until those are changed to xsd:dateTime or a custom datatype is declared. With that substitution the ontology and its example data are consistent and contain no unsatisfiable classes.

Imported-term narrowing. ORDA asserts domains, ranges and restrictions on terms it does not define, including dct:format, dct:hasFormat, dct:identifier, several frapo properties, frapo:Funding and several dpv properties. These axioms propagate to anyone importing ORDA, and are stronger than the source vocabularies assert. Importers who need the unconstrained terms should import the source vocabulary directly.

One case is worth calling out because it is a typing rather than a restriction. dct:description is declared here as an owl:DatatypeProperty, which DCMI does not do: it publishes the term as a plain rdf:Property. ORDA needs the typing so that orda:independenceDeclaration and its siblings can be sub-properties of it in OWL 2 DL. The term is given no domain, so nothing is constrained about what may be described; but data that uses dct:description with a resource rather than a literal value will fall outside OWL 2 DL when merged with ORDA.

Scoped out of this draft, and named here so their absence is not mistaken for an oversight: assessment decision records and rejection grounds; the procedural deadlines in DA Art. 7, Art. 40(5) and DA Art. 13; dispute settlement bodies; research outputs and publication records beyond the commitment flag.
